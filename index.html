01 — RESEARCH & DATA VISUALIZATION

Fear on the Road

Mapping how people actually feel about a private road in Los Gatos.

Overlook road is a private road that is located in Los Gatos. With this project, I wanted to find out and visualize people's perception and experience the road in a unique way.

Let's interview

06People interviewed
10Questions each
45Days in the field

OBJECTIVES

  • Share experiences that people have had on the Overlook Road in an interesting way.
  • Come up with an easily understandable method of communicating that information.

FINDINGS

  • A portion of people who use the Overlook Road had experiences where they felt fear.
  • The fear that people felt varied a lot in scale and source of cause.
  • Each source had a unique story or event associated with it.
  • The road was perceived very differently from day to night.

Final poster — each marker sized and coloured by fear level, with the interview quote that placed it

By creating an abstract line graphic that represents the layout of the Overlook road and mapping the fear level on a scale using simple circle graphics, I created a visually striking poster that shares the unnerving stories that people have shared about the road. By using a dark background and an unusual neon color pallet I wanted to convey the general scary perception that people have of this road. I added quotes from the interview for further context and detail.

GETTING THERE — IDEATION SHEET

"The road could be difficult and scary to navigate at night, due to it having no street lights or easily visible borders. Encounters with cars or wild life are always unpleasant."

02 — UX / PRODUCT DESIGN · TEAM PROJECT

SF Recycle Plus

Making "is this recyclable?" a five-second question, not a guess.

Recycling has always been marketed as something good for the environment, but not everyone knows how to properly recycle. Through careful research, my team and I were tasked with finding and proposing a solution that would help build towards a more environmentally conscious future.

The brief

OBJECTIVES

  • Gather data through one-on-one interviews about recycling habits and challenges that people face.
  • Propose a solution that promptly addresses the common underlying issues.
  • Create a fully functioning prototype that aims to aid in users' recycling journey.

We interviewed 16 people

We gathered data about users' recycling habits and challenges through one-on-one interviews. Getting to know how SF residents feel about recycling gave us a better understanding of what problems need to be addressed.

THE PROBLEM

33% of Californian residents are unsure how to recycle properly. This contributes to 79% of our recyclable products going into the landfill.

What challenges do you face when trying to recycle?

Once the data was gathered, it was time to convert it into usable statistics that would guide the team to finding a solution. Through careful organization of the data, we divided the feedback into three distinctive categories, split by the type of roadblock preventing people from recycling. We then took note of how easy or difficult each one is to address, and used that to determine the focus priority for the project.

The first low-fi home screen focused on informing the users through related articles. The main camera scanning feature was accessible via navigation panel on the bottom.

Time to reapproach and apply user feedback

That feedback resolved into three themes, and each one rewrote a different part of the screen.

03 — BRAND & PACKAGING

Tazo Tea Box

Designing a classic line for an already-iconic brand.

This project was focused on designing a product line for an existing tea brand called Tazo. It was important to keep in mind the current brand identity and create a product line that stands out but is also visually coherent with the rest of Tazo’s boxes.

Learning the market

Before coming up with a design, it was necessary to get a clear understanding of the competitors in the market. This research gives insight in to potential growth opportunities for the Tazo brand.

OBJECTIVES

  • Make a new product line for the Tazo brand.
  • Maintain the current brand identity.
  • Make a standout product line.

FINDINGS

  • Tazo as it stands is missing a line of tea boxes that offers simple options to the customer.
  • Tazo tea box designs focus on simple, bold, and modern branding.
  • Tea box layouts have certain FDA guidelines that are required to be fulfilled.

Market research analysis

A SWOT read on the brand, split across what Tazo already does well, where it is exposed, and what a new line could realistically claim.

Strengths
  • Consistent modern style branding
  • Effective mission statement
  • Environmental conservation (Treecorps)
  • Variety of interesting tea flavor options
  • Both hot and cold tea products
Opportunities
  • Possible to integrate the environmental conservation goal into the packaging itself
  • Have premium versions of tea lines
  • More variety in packaging design while keeping the general theme consistent
Weaknesses
  • Poor interactivity within the website navigation bar
  • While the packaging is consistent, it feels too similar
  • Upon first glance it’s not clear that they have environmental conservation (Treecorps) as a focus
  • Higher end price tag
Threats
  • Competitors with similar modern design but at a lower price range
  • Competitor brands with a more clear environmental conservation messaging

Designing the line

Each flavour got its own mark, built from the ingredient itself and the two words people most often reach for when describing that tea. The petal geometry stays constant across the three so the family reads as one line on shelf.

The final design

The importance of proper research

Throughout this project, there were a lot of important takeaways. Mainly, how truly important detailed research is to come up with a unique design that is in line with Tazo's brand values and fits in with the rest of the Tazo tea boxes.

Without a proper establishment for the current state of the Tazo brand as well as its competitors, it would have been extremely difficult to come up with a cohesive design that fit the objective of the project. Going forward, I want to approach every research phase with extra care and attention to detail to ensure that I have enough necessary information to work with.

04 — PACKAGING SYSTEM

Poppy Soda

One label system, six flavors, zero confusion at the shelf.

A solo project built around a simple constraint: six very different fruit flavours, one label system, and no confusion at the shelf. Each flavour draws its palette from the real fruit, held together by a trio of circles standing in for carbonation.

Learning the market

Seeing common trends and analyzing the preexisting brands in the current soda market, allowed me to find key features that a successful soda packaging design should have. There were 3 notable patterns.

OBJECTIVES

  • Make a colour system for 6 different flavours.
  • Develop a cohesive design system.

FINDINGS

  • Successful soda product lines use bold shapes to increase recognizability.
  • Simple colour palettes can communicate the flavour of the product without any other visual indication.
  • A cohesive design system across a line of products helps customers better identify brands.

Ideation

Design system for a coherent collection
